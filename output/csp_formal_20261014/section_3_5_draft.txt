3.5. Comparison with a Constraint-Based Solver

We evaluated a constraint-based solver adapted from nabenabe0928/mine-sweeper-solver (commit 3a499ddee44bf7562808c8ab787bd173fb68fa2e; Apache-2.0). It uses only revealed clues, unknown-cell locations, and the public mine count. Deterministic propagation establishes safe cells and mines; otherwise, feasible frontier configurations are enumerated and weighted by the number of compatible non-frontier mine placements. The solver selects the lowest-risk unknown cell, with reproducible row-major tie breaking.

Enumeration is limited to 20,000 iterations per decision, without a wall-clock cutoff. Incomplete counts are discarded if the budget is exceeded, and a logged heuristic risk estimate is used. Exact uniform probabilities in states without frontier constraints are recorded separately. We used 10,000 games per mine count on 9 x 9 boards, grouped into ten consecutive batches of 1,000 games. Episode seeds were (20261013 + episode index) modulo 2^32. The first click was the top-left cell, with the original safe-but-not-necessarily-zero rule and terminal conditions. This is a unified evaluation protocol, not verified board-by-board pairing with the previously reported CNN runs.

The constraint-based solver achieved 90.04% for 10 mines; 74.22% for 14 mines; 47.17% for 18 mines; 20.73% for 22 mines. Wilson confidence intervals, evaluation-group standard deviations, and search-budget diagnostics are supplied with the formal results. At 18 mines, its measured win rate was 47.17%, compared with 20.38% for the evaluated SCL online-best checkpoint. The corresponding SP comparison must be inserted from documented SP measurements; no SP value is inferred here.

This comparison distinguishes explicit constraint reasoning from staged CNN training. The contribution of SCL is the training strategy within the evaluated CNN family, rather than a general claim of superiority to traditional solvers. Evaluation-group variability does not establish robustness across independent training seeds. Best-checkpoint and fixed-budget endpoint comparisons should also be distinguished.
